Meet the satellite

NISAR: a radar that works through clouds

0days to revisit a spot
L + Sradar bands (L is used here)

NISAR is a joint NASA and ISRO mission. Its long L-band wavelength reaches deeper into forest than Sentinel-1’s C-band, so it senses trunks and branches as well as leaves. Radar makes its own light, so it sees day or night and through the Amazon’s clouds.

A short history

How NISAR got to Manaus

2007
US scientists recommend a radar mission to watch land and ice change.
2014
NASA and ISRO agree to build one satellite together.
30 Jul 2025
Launch from India. NISAR then spends its first months in checkout.
Oct 2025
The first scenes appear in the public catalog.
Jun 2026
Continuous coverage over Manaus begins (scenes since 23 Jun).
6 Nov 2026
Validated products start, covering scenes since 21 Oct. This is why the team’s planned pair is 21 Oct and 2 Nov.

Dates for 2007, 2014 and the launch come from general mission history. Dates from Oct 2025 onward come from the team’s base document. Confirm both at the official NISAR source before publishing.
